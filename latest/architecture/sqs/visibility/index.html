    Visibility

    A received SQS message stays in the queue, invisible for the visibility timeout, until a delete ends it or it comes back for another receive.

    A receive hands the message out with a receipt handle and hides it from every other receive for the visibility timeout. The timeout is the queue's own, 30 seconds by default and up to 12 hours, unless the receive names one. SQS counts it in whole seconds, from one second up. The consumer deletes the message with the receipt handle of that receive.

    A message that is not deleted in time becomes visible again and is delivered once more. ChangeMessageVisibility sets a new timeout, counted from the call. A timeout of zero makes the message visible at once. A timeout that would end more than 12 hours after the receive is refused.

    Every receive adds one to the message's ApproximateReceiveCount. A queue with a redrive policy names a dead-letter queue and a maxReceiveCount from 1 to 1000. A message received that many times without a delete is moved to the dead-letter queue on its next receive, instead of being delivered. The dead-letter queue is of the same kind as the source: a FIFO queue takes a FIFO dead-letter queue.

    SQS has no lease to renew. While a handler holds a delivery, this crate calls ChangeMessageVisibility halfway through each timeout, so a slow handler is not raced by a second delivery of the same message. The other half is left for the call to reach the queue. The extension stops when the delivery settles or is dropped. A queue whose timeout is zero gets no extension.

    The 12 hours from the receive bound the extension too. A handler that holds a delivery that long gets one last extension to the end of them, and the crate logs a warning. After that the queue hands the message to another consumer.

    The behaviour is Amazon SQS's own (visibility timeout, ChangeMessageVisibility, dead-letter queues).
